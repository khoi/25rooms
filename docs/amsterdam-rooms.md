# Amsterdam: Water outside, a whole life inside

Twenty-five miniature interiors follow Amsterdam from a rain-covered family bicycle to a last lit attic. The canal district supplies narrow thresholds and deep windows, while Noord, NDSM, Oost, Oud-West, Nieuw-West, De Pijp, and Zuidoost bring different scales of housing, work, recreation, and cultural life. The visual thread is the contrast between moving water or weather outside and carefully fitted, intensely inhabited space inside.

These are 25 contemporary, fictional room proposals. Citations support the stated local connections, not exact interiors, businesses, occupants, or production methods. The sequence follows changing light across separate days. Seasonal choices describe the scene, not the weather on the research date. No opening hours, current service schedules, or access permissions are implied.

Use the existing roughly 12-by-12 isometric footprint, two open front edges, printed outlines, and paper, blue, teal, coral, and sun palette. At collection scale, preserve the dominant shape; at normal zoom, reveal the human activity and purposeful clusters; at close view, expose wear and personal discoveries. Keep landmarks within a narrow exterior strip or window. All artwork stays free of lettering, including packaging, diagrams, covers, and equipment displays. Use ordinary contemporary clothing appropriate to the activity.

The visual benchmark is the original collection's library, kitchen, print shop, and greenhouse: deep fitted boundaries, substantial working assemblies, and small objects connected to use. Each room below specifies two developed assemblies, a spatial envelope, a human relationship, a timed reveal, and material behavior at several heights. The discoveries extend the main activity into personal history, maintenance, and quiet visual surprises. For each assembly, expose working surfaces, supports, connections, and recessed contents. Moving pieces need visible pivots, stops, and clearance. Attach small objects to plausible supports, and vary stored objects' orientation. Put wear where hands, feet, tools, and weather cause it. All new construction details are original design proposals within the cited local context.

At collection size, the first-impression sentence defines the dominant shape and light contrast. At normal size, the principal activity and both assemblies must read without zooming. At close size, reveal interiors, joints, material edges, repairs, and the relationships between stored objects. Keep roughly a quarter of the useful floor available for movement or visual rest where the setting allows it. Large blank faces need a structural reason; repeated texture cannot substitute for construction.

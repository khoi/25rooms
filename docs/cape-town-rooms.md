# Cape Town: A room with the wind outside

The collection connects the coast, mountain-facing suburbs, central workshops, and Cape Flats neighbourhoods through ordinary work and shared rooms. Langa and Khayelitsha have creative, domestic, educational, and recreational scenes. They are inhabited communities, not visual shorthand for deprivation. Wind and water influence useful things: screens, drying rails, shelter, and the way a door is held.

These are 25 contemporary, fictional room proposals. Citations support the stated local connections, not exact interiors, businesses, occupants, or production methods. The sequence follows changing light across separate days. Seasonal choices describe the scene, not the weather on the research date. No opening hours, current service schedules, or access permissions are implied.

Keep landmarks within a narrow exterior strip or window. All artwork stays free of lettering, including packaging, diagrams, covers, and equipment displays. Use ordinary contemporary clothing appropriate to the activity.

The visual benchmark is the original collection's library, kitchen, print shop, and greenhouse: deep fitted boundaries, substantial working assemblies, and small objects connected to use. Each room below has two developed assemblies and eight purposeful areas, with detail at floor, hand, shelf, and upper-wall heights. Seven discoveries extend the main activity into personal history, maintenance, and quiet visual surprises. For each assembly, expose working surfaces, supports, connections, and recessed contents.

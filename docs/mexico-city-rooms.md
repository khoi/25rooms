# Mexico City: Courtyards, water, and working hands

A collection that connects the capital's dense central streets to its southern cultivation and eastern community spaces. Volcanic stone, shaded patios, covered markets, classrooms, and small rooftops create distinct volumes. Food and craft occupy part of the tour, alongside sport, care, transport, study, and ordinary home life.

These are 25 contemporary, fictional room proposals. Citations support the stated local connections, not exact interiors, businesses, occupants, or production methods. The sequence follows changing light across separate days. Seasonal choices describe the scene, not the weather on the research date. No opening hours, current service schedules, or access permissions are implied.

Use the existing roughly 12-by-12 isometric footprint, two open front edges, printed outlines, and paper, blue, teal, coral, and sun palette. At collection scale, preserve the dominant shape; at normal zoom, reveal the human activity and five clusters; at close view, expose wear and personal discoveries. Keep landmarks within a narrow exterior strip or window. All artwork stays free of lettering, including packaging, diagrams, covers, and equipment displays. Use ordinary contemporary clothing appropriate to the activity.

## Tour order and review

The tour opens where food and plants enter the city, then crosses shared thresholds into domestic, commercial, and cultural work. The middle widens into learning and recreation in eastern and northern districts. Later rooms return to the water and then to roofs and kitchens. The two cultivation rooms separate soil and plant inspection from seed storage; the boat room studies a moored vessel, not an interchangeable tourist ride. An original small bird made from scrap card recurs in the carton studio, library, and final home.

The order is a sequence of visits, not a literal same-day route. Compare the five-room bands as morning preparation, public activity, concentrated work, shared afternoon space, and evening return. Each row has a different combination of setting, silhouette, activity, and object set. Similar trades use different focal gestures and fixtures; the numbered sequence is the proposed collection order.

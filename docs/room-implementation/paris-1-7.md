# Paris rooms 1–7 implementation review

All seven modules are independent room definitions under `dist/artwork/cities/paris/`. They retain the 12 × 12 footprint and established palette. The seven compositions use an oven hall, glazed booth and passage, curved cabin, produce canopy, river parapet, attic roof and repair courtyard. Local articulated people keep their hands attached to scene-specific targets; they do not register or overwrite shared figure clips.

The first bakery render was compared at 800 pixels with the original kitchen, library and press in `/tmp/paris-1-7/prototype-comparison.png`. Its oven silhouette and working diagonal read clearly, but the first version lacked developed service fittings and workbench detail. The revision added the vent collar and pipe, oven controls and masonry courses, workbench scale, rolling pin, tool sheath, drawer faces and flour brush. The originals retain stronger atmospheric light and denser incidental relationships; this review does not claim the new rooms surpass them.

Visual evidence is outside the repository in `/tmp/paris-1-7/`. `collection.png` shows every room at 220 pixels. Every room has a separate `<slug>-normal.png` at 800 pixels, `<slug>-close.png` at 1400 pixels, and `<slug>-motion.png` with six timed frames. All seven collection images, normal views, close views, intended still poses and motion sheets were inspected. Final refinements corrected hand reach, a window overlap and crossing sightlines.

Validation used direct module imports and the real drawing helpers, wrapping shape operations to reject empty and nonfinite coordinate arrays. All seven static layers and ten sampled live states per room passed, including each main beat, the declared still time and both sides of the loop boundary. Native RoomPainter renders also passed an exact pixel comparison of animation time zero with the full loop duration for all seven rooms, excluding only the external screenshot caption. No shared files, registry, existing rooms or helper exports were changed by this worker.

Final browser integration and collection navigation are owned by the coordinating agent. The artwork uses simple illustrated faces and hands; the native image review establishes composition and contact, not an anatomy simulation.

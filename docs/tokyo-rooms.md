**Tokyo, room by room**

A proposed collection of 25 animated isometric scenes for 25rooms. The collection follows Tokyo from early work to late nights, with visits to homes, workshops, shops, places of worship, and shared public spaces.

The rooms are fictional compositions informed by real neighborhoods. Their layouts, occupants, possessions, and small stories are original design proposals. Named places establish the setting without implying an exact architectural reconstruction. Spring blossoms and summer river outings belong to separate moments across the year. The sequence follows the changing light, rather than a literal itinerary on one day.

The existing 12-by-12 room footprint is the composition unit. Most scenes retain two rear walls and open front edges. Courtyards, a train carriage, a boat cabin, and a rooftop use equivalent boundaries. A distant landmark remains inside a window, skyline panel, or narrow background layer. It never becomes a second full-sized scene.

Each room has a large shape that reads at collection scale, a human activity that becomes clear on approach, and small discoveries at close range. The prop lists describe depth, rather than equal visual emphasis. Around one quarter of the visible floor stays clear where circulation or the setting allows it. Foreground objects remain low enough to expose the main action.

The artwork keeps the existing paper, blue, teal, coral, and sun palette, with tone changes suggesting different materials. Cool white rooms use paper and pale blue. Amber interiors use sun over coral. Steel uses blue edges and paper highlights. Lantern ribs, cut glass, tatami borders, roof tiles, and train fittings carry cultural detail through shape. Artwork remains free of lettering. Names and explanations belong in the viewer caption, while shop displays use silhouettes and color blocks rather than invented Japanese text.

The proposed animation durations below describe comfortable visual rhythms. Each scene has one main action and a few quieter environmental movements. A calm still composition must also work for reduced motion. The collection uses ordinary contemporary clothing appropriate to each activity. Traditional garments appear in specific contexts, such as sumo practice. Recurring possessions provide continuity: a coral umbrella, a blue cloth book bag, and a small original bird character appear in selected rooms.
